## Pillars

### GrpProsp: Group Prospects

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: shared. Axis: Prospects

How far you feel the group, as it now stands, can pull off the venture it has taken on and not yet finished: a game, project, season or campaign. It is confidence in the capability the venture will run on, not luck, not your own prospects (Payoff), not whether each will do their part (Dependability) and not a general sense that the group is strong. The culture's face sets where that capability is looked for.

- 10: You feel sure the group, as it stands, can pull off what it has set itself; the venture looks well within its reach.
- 1: You feel sure the group, as it stands, cannot pull off what it has set itself; the venture looks beyond it.
- Pre-language: A felt sense, before any words, that this band can bring off what it is setting out to do: chimpanzee males who hear strangers chorus and advance together when their party holds three or more males, and stay silent or hold back when it holds fewer (Wilson et al. 2001); and the chimpanzee who picks as partner the one who has made a joint task work before (Melis et al. 2006). Intimates read it from her bearing as the venture starts: settled and forward-leaning, in step with the others, or hesitant and watchful, eyes on the others rather than the task.

Face in the egalitarian deck: Capability spread across the members. How far you feel the group, as it now stands, can pull off the venture it has agreed to take on and not yet finished. It is confidence in the group's capability for that venture, spread across its members, their skill included, and including the skill of whoever the band looks to for this venture for the day. Whether that member pushes others about is Parity's ground, and whether the others look to you is Esteem's. It is not luck, not your own prospects (Payoff), not whether each will do their part (Dependability), and not a general sense that the group is strong.
- 10: You feel sure the group, as it stands, can pull off what it has set itself; the venture looks well within its reach.
- 1: You feel sure the group, as it stands, cannot pull off what it has set itself; the venture looks beyond it.

Face in the hierarchical deck: Confidence in those who steer. How far you feel those at the top of the order, yourself included if you are there, can steer the group through the venture it has taken on and not yet finished: read the ground, choose the course, set each member to the part that suits them and right the course when it goes wrong. It reads their competence to direct this venture only. It is not whether they deal evenly with you, hearing you, holding you to the same rules and serving no favourites first (Parity), not the deference you draw for what you bring (Esteem), not your rung in the order (Rank), not what staying will bring you (Payoff), and not whether those below will carry their parts (Dependability). A well-meaning leader out of their depth rates low; a hard one who knows the way rates high.
- 10: You feel sure those at the top know the way through what the group has taken on; you follow their lead without second-guessing it, and if you are among them you feel the course is in safe hands.
- 1: You feel sure those at the top cannot steer the group through what it has taken on; each call they make looks like the wrong one, and if you are among them you feel the course slipping out of your hands.
- Pre-language: A felt sense, before any words, that the ones in front know where to take the band: the troop's readiness to rise and follow when the lead animal moves off, as subordinate chacma baboons followed the dominant male's foraging moves even at a cost to themselves (King et al. 2008), against hanging back, lagging or drifting off when the lead's moves have come to nothing. Read with care: in King's study following depended on the followers' social bonds with the leader, so it rests on ties as well as on any reading of where he leads; and in olive baboons movement is settled among many initiators and the dominant is not simply followed (Strandburg-Peshkin et al. 2015). Following a single leader is therefore one primate pattern among several, and the model borrows it for ranked orders only. Intimates read it from how she meets a call from the top: moving with it at once, eyes on the task, or a pause, a glance at the others and a slow, half-hearted start; and, if she is at the front herself, from a sure, unhurried move off against a false start and a look back.

Effect on Engagement: That is likely to succeed in goals that you believe are worth pursing, or you would not have joined the group in the first place, and that has agreed goals, or otherwise random changes to the group's direction will move outside the model's ability to predict.

### OwnProsp: Own Prospects

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: HCP. Axis: Prospects

How likely you feel it is that staying in this group will get you what you yourself are after from it: the place, play, pay, skill, advancement or mate you joined for. It is a forecast of what the group will deliver to you, not your own ability, not your present rung in the order (Rank), not the deference you draw now for what you bring (Esteem), not whether the others want you among them now (Belonging), not the group's own venture (Team Confidence), not whether help you give comes back (Reciprocity), not whether anyone takes more than their due or tilts a share-out against you now (Parity), and not a tally of what it has given so far. Each of these can feed the forecast without being it.

- 10: You are sure that staying will bring you what you came for; the group's dealings with you keep opening the way to it.
- 1: You are sure that staying will bring you nothing you came for; whatever you do here, the way to it stays shut.
- Pre-language: The felt yield of staying with this band for her: whether being here keeps bringing her food, a mate, a place or a skill, or keeps bringing her nothing. Intimates read it from whether she settles in and works at her own aims here, or drifts to the edge, looks outward and leaves.

Effect on Engagement: Where you feel that you are likely to achieve your personal goals, whether or not they are part of the group goals (Team Confidence) or indeed, conflict with them. Presumably if personal and group goals are in conflict, you would prefer to have low levels of Team Confidence however such a condition is out of scope for the model as it indicates deception and potentially sabotage, which is a cognitive rather than an unconscious drive (as the Pillars are contended to be).

### Status: Status

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: HCP. Axis: Standing

Your felt rung in the group's settled order of precedence: how far you go first, take the first turn and the first share as a matter of course, and how far you go after others and take what is left. All recognise the order, so it is not fought out each time. The precedence attaches to your place, however you came by it: an office, seniority, the backing of kin or allies, an old contest no one reopens, or credit for what you bring that has settled, over time, into a place others take for granted. It is distinct from the live deference you draw for what you bring now (Esteem), and once settled it does not need present fear to hold it day to day (Parity), though an old contest or the backing of allies may stand behind it.

- 10: You go first as a matter of course: the first turn, the first share and the best seat are yours without a word said, and no one in the group stands above you.
- 1: You go last as a matter of course: you wait your turn and take your share and seat after everyone else, without a word said, and no one in the group stands below you.
- Pre-language: Her felt rung in an order all recognise: the others let her pass, eat and settle first and greet her with the calm signals of submission, or she passes, eats and settles after them and greets them so, and neither side has to fight it out each time. Intimates read it from who passes first when paths cross, who eats first, who takes the best spot, and who greets whom.

Effect on Engagement: Where you feel that you possess formal power within a group, enabling you to utilise the group to achieve your own vision, which aligns the group with your identity. This is why new leaders often revoke measures taken by the preceding.

### NormExp: Normative Expression (norm guarding)

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: HCP. Axis: Expression

How strongly it strikes you as wrong when another member breaks from the group's settled ways in a way the group would condemn, in act or by openly pressing to change them. It is felt indignation at a breach, not dislike of the person. Intimates read it from whether you pull the member up. An objection, a rebuke or mockery are anger's forms: they look for the member to mend their ways and be taken back. Where the breach repeats or reads as bad character, a cold shoulder or shutting them out are contempt's forms, and they last. The reading's level does not set the form; the form sets how long the response bites and whether it ends in reconciliation or exclusion. Your own conformity and your openness to ideas are not part of it. Among equals the group's ways include its levelling ways: not presuming on success or boasting, not keeping back what should be shared, and pulling your weight; a breach of these stirs the same bristle, and the band answers it with ribbing and complaint (Boehm 1999; Wiessner 2005). Whether such a member lords it over you yourself is Parity's ground. Whether their holding back leaves your own need unmet is Reciprocity's, and whether you can count on them to do their part is Dependability's; the bristle here answers the breach, whoever it wrongs.

- 10: The smallest departure the group would condemn jars you at once as plainly wrong, and intimates see you pull the member up on the spot: a sharp word or a mocking laugh, or a cold shoulder where the breach repeats or reads as bad character.
- 1: A member breaking the group's settled ways, or openly pressing to change them, leaves you unmoved even where the group would condemn it; nothing in you says it is wrong, and intimates see you let it pass.
- Pre-language: The bristle at one of the band doing a thing the wrong way. Its sign is the move to pull them up: a protest cry, a shove, a cold shoulder or exclusion. In one theft experiment, captive chimpanzees retaliated for their own losses but did not punish theft from others (Riedl et al. 2012). Intimates read it from how she meets another's departure: with indignation and a rebuke, or with a shrug. Forager bands such as the Ju/'hoansi turn a like bristle, by criticism and mockery, on upstarts and stingy members (Wiessner 2005); in this model that egalitarian form is carried by Safety->Parity and stands here only as the contrast. The face here is the guarding of a ranked group's settled ways.

Effect on Engagement: Where you feel that you can manage (minimise unwelcome or surprising) change, by using your power to reduce any propensity for others to suggest change. While high Rule-Keeping undermines Psychological Safety for others, it makes you personally feel safer.

### OutResp: Outgoing Respect

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: ECP. Axis: Respect

How far you feel the other members can be counted on to do their part in the shared work: each carries through what they take on and keeps their word about it, so you leave work in their hands without checking that they are doing it. It is reliability, not skill (Team Confidence), and not whether a slip or weak spot you show would be used against you (Safety).

- 10: You leave any part of the work with any member and never check whether they are doing it; what they take on gets done and what they promise about it holds.
- 1: You check up on and chase others' work and redo what they skimp, expecting them to shirk, cut corners or go back on their word.
- Pre-language: Whether she can turn her back on them at a shared task. One who counts on her fellows leaves the watch, the carry or the young in their hands and goes about her own business without looking back; one who does not keeps glancing over, hovers at their work or takes it over herself. Intimates read it from how often she looks back, checks and takes over. The mechanism is trust calibrated to a partner's record of carrying a joint task, the kind of partner assessment the interdependence account places early in human cooperation (Tomasello et al. 2012). What sets this Pillar apart from Reciprocity is what the record is of: the partner's share of a joint task, not a favour returned to her. It is her reading of them; whether they turn their backs on her is a different thing and not read here.

Effect on Engagement: With competent and trustworthy people because you like being associated with 'winners', you admire and wish to model yourself off them, and they can help you to improve.

### Levelling: Levelling

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: shared. Axis: Standing

How far you feel no one in the group sets themselves over you unfairly: by force or fear, by taking more than their due and keeping it, by tilting how shares, turns and chances are handed out, who gets a hearing, or how a quarrel is settled, towards themselves or their favourites, or by treating you with contempt. The culture's face sets where that threat is looked for: among equals, a would-be boss; in a ranked group, partial or overbearing dealing from those above.

- 10: No one in the group makes you comply by force or threat, takes more than their due and keeps it, or tilts a share-out, a hearing or the settling of a quarrel against you; you take your portion and go about your business without watching anyone warily.
- 1: One or a few in the group make you comply through force or fear, take more than their due and keep it, or decide and divide in favour of their own while you are passed over or go unheard; you watch them warily and let them have their way, because objecting gets you nowhere or draws their anger.
- Pre-language: Freedom from a bully she must appease. The free animal moves, feeds and takes her share in the strongest member's presence without flinching or a glance at that member's mood; the coerced one tracks that member's moods, freezes on approach and does whatever heads off an attack. Intimates read it from whether she watches one particular member warily and shrinks or placates when that member comes near, while staying easy with everyone else, and whether, at a share-out, she is handed her portion in turn or watches the one dividing it feed his friends first and pass her by.

Face in the egalitarian deck: No one lords it over you. How far you feel no one in the group sets themselves over you: makes you do, drop or endure things by force or fear; takes more than the group's ordinary share-out gives them, of food, credit or say, and makes it stick; tilts how shares, turns and chances are handed out, or how a quarrel is settled, towards themselves or their favourites, passing you over; or treats you with contempt, as if above you. You go along of your own accord, unafraid, not shortchanged and not passed over. The reaction that lowers it takes one of two forms, depending on whether you could answer the wrong. Alone against force, it is wariness and fear, and you keep away or appease. With others who would back you, or where the wrong is a grab or a tilted share-out rather than a threat, it is aggrieved anger, and you want to confront them. Either form lowers the reading by the same amount; they differ in what you then do and in which cards bite. Where you could move to another group at little cost, a would-be boss has less hold over you, and the same push lowers this reading less; where leaving is costly, it lowers it more. It is not rank (Rank), acceptance (Belonging), deference for what you bring (Esteem), the return on help or whether those with a surplus of their own share it on demand (Reciprocity), whether others do their part of the work (Dependability), the cost of showing yourself (Safety), the others' verdict on your own conduct (Repute) or whether a charge against your character stands before those who count (Face). A respected leader, a settled order you accept, an even-handed share-out that happens to leave you less this time, or the group together pulling up a breach of its ways leaves it high; a member you must appease, one who grabs and gets away with it, or one who hands out or settles in favour of their own lowers it. Where the group has spoken ways of deciding, having those ways applied to you as to anyone, and a fair hearing for your say, feed the same reading even when the outcome goes against you; a say that is heard and then brushed aside, never weighed, does not raise it.
- 10: No member or clique can make you do or drop anything by force or threat, no one takes more than their due and keeps it, and whoever hands out or settles things does so with no thumb on the scale. You move, take your share and go about your business in the strongest member's presence as freely as in anyone's.
- 1: One or a few members make you comply through force or fear, take more than their due and keep it, or hand out and settle things in favour of their own while you are passed over. Alone against them, you watch them warily, fall quiet when they come near, and let them have their way to head off their anger. Where others would back you, or the wrong is a grab or a tilt rather than a threat, it galls you and you bristle at them, though you may still let it pass because objecting gets you nowhere.
- Pre-language: Freedom from a bully she must appease. The free animal moves, feeds and takes her share in the strongest member's presence without flinching or a glance at that member's mood; the coerced one tracks that member's moods, freezes on approach and does whatever heads off an attack. Intimates read it from whether she watches one particular member warily and shrinks or placates when that member comes near, while staying easy with everyone else, and whether, at a share-out, she is handed her portion in turn or watches the one dividing it feed his friends first and pass her by.

Face in the hierarchical deck: Even hands from those above. How far you feel those above you in the order, and the order's own ways of deciding, deal evenly with you: they hear you before deciding what touches you, hold you to the same rules as anyone at your rung, hand out shares, turns and chances without serving their favourites first, settle quarrels without a thumb on the scale, give orders only as far as the rules of your place allow, without threat or fear, and treat you with the courtesy your place is owed. It is not your rung (Rank), not whether partners repay you (Reciprocity), not whether those at the top can steer (Team Confidence), not deference for what you bring (Esteem), not the cost of showing yourself (Safety), not whether you are kept among them (Belonging) and not whether a charge against your character stands before those who count (Face), though being denied a hearing on such a charge lowers this reading. A settled order that gives you less by rank but applies its rules evenly and hears you leaves it high; a decision made over your head, a patron who serves his own first, a superior who rules by threat, or contempt from above lowers it.
- 10: Those above you hear you out before any call that touches you, apply the same rules to you as to anyone at your rung and hand out turns and shares with no favourite served first; you take your portion when it comes without watching the one who divides it.
- 1: Calls that touch you are made over your head, favourites are served before you whatever the rules say, and objecting gets you waved off or met with a threat; you watch whoever divides, settles and gives orders with a wary, aggrieved eye.
- Pre-language: Whether the one who divides and settles hands her portion in its turn and hears her out, or feeds his favourites first and waves her off. Intimates read it from whether she waits at the share-out at ease or watches the divider warily, and whether she puts her case to those above or no longer bothers. After a clash with one above her, a soft grunt or easy approach from him tells her it is over and she stays near; a hard stare sends her off. When those above break up fights without taking sides, she feeds and rests among them at ease.

### DivsExp: Diverse Expression (felt safety)

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: ECP. Axis: Expression

How safe you feel to show yourself in this group: to let a slip, a mood, a need or a disagreement show, or go about things your own way, expecting no put-down, mockery or rebuff for it. It includes whether a slip, a need or a weak spot you let show would be turned against you to get ahead of you or to do you harm. It is your reading of what showing yourself will cost you, not whether you speak up; not whether a need you show gets met (Reciprocity), whether your word carries weight (Esteem) or whether you are wanted (Belonging); not whether anyone pushes you about by force or fear, whether those who decide hear you out before a call that touches you or whether those above treat you with contempt whatever you show (Parity); not the verdict already held on your conduct (Repute) or being openly charged or caught out before those who count (Face); and not your own indignation at others' departures (Rule-Keeping).

- 10: You feel free to let slips, moods, needs and disagreements show as they come and to go about things your own way, expecting no one to pounce on them, laugh at them or turn away.
- 1: You feel that letting a slip, a mood, a plain need, a disagreement or a way of your own show would draw a put-down or be used against you, so you scan faces before you move and keep your reactions masked.
- Pre-language: Relaxed against braced among her own: the loose body and open play of an animal that expects no bite from its fellows, against the scanning, tense stillness of one that does. Intimates read it from how loose or tight she is when eyes turn to her, and from whether she hides a hurt or a want.

Effect on Engagement: Where you feel that your ideas will be welcomed and well-received. While related to IncResp (HCP), Expression is closer to a norm of Psychological Safety than being given specific respect for one's competence. Such a norm creates a warm, less hostile environment, which is distinct from cohesion: a close, friendly group can still make challenge feel risky.

### Reciprocity: Reciprocity

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: shared. Axis: Reciprocity

Your felt reading of whether help in this group comes back: what you give is returned, what you ask for is met. It holds until evidence moves it. It concerns the return on help, not whether others carry their part of shared work (Dependability), whether anyone takes more of a common take than their due (Parity), your indignation at a member who keeps back what should be shared (Rule-Keeping), where you stand (Rank), whether the others want you among them (Belonging) or how they judge your conduct (Repute, Face), though your good name feeds the return. The culture's face sets where the return is looked for.

- 10: What you give comes back and what you ask for is met, as a matter of course; you count on the return without a second thought.
- 1: What you give is not returned and what you need goes unmet; you expect nothing back from anyone here.
- Pre-language: The felt return on sharing: grooming that is returned by those groomed (Schino and Aureli 2008), and forager food handed over on demand to whoever asks (Peterson 1993), with those who have given tending to be given to in turn (Allen-Arave, Gurven and Hill 2008). Intimates read it as a sign in her behaviour: whether she hands over on request and asks as of right, expecting the return, or hesitates, hoards and goes without rather than ask.

Face in the egalitarian deck: Return from the group. Help goes to whoever needs it, and the return comes from the group at large. Big windfalls go out largely by demand and tolerated taking, to whoever is there, whatever their record; what tracks being seen to share is the smaller, everyday giving and the care a member gets when sick or hurt. Whoever has a surplus is pressed to share on demand, and asking is as normal as giving. Your reading is whether, when you are in need, the group at large gives to you.
- 10: Whoever has gives to whoever needs; what you have put in comes back to you from any member when you are in need.
- 1: Giving goes into a hole: nothing comes back from anyone, those with a surplus hide it, and need goes unmet.
- Pre-language: Food handed over on demand within the band, with the return coming from whoever next has a surplus; those who hide food lose standing and are passed over. Intimates read it as a sign in her behaviour: whether she hands her surplus to anyone who asks and asks anyone who has, or holds back from both.

Face in the hierarchical deck: Return from the partner. Help flows between particular partners who return it to each other. It is conditional on a return from the same partner: in kind between equals, or across rank, as favour and protection passed down for loyalty and service passed up. Your reading is whether your partners pay you back, not where you stand (Rank), whether they do their part of the shared work (Dependability), whether those above decide evenly (Parity) or whether your character stands unchallenged before those who count (Face), though being shown up as one who leaves a favour unreturned lowers Face, and Face in turn feeds the return.
- 10: Every partner you help pays you back and those you ask deliver; whoever stands above you covers you and whoever stands below you backs you, each in turn.
- 1: Partners take and do not return; whoever stands above you leaves you uncovered and whoever stands below you will not back you; you expect no return from anyone you deal with.
- Pre-language: Grooming returned by the one groomed (Schino and Aureli 2008). Intimates read it as a sign in her behaviour: whether she keeps giving to those who gave to her and turns from those who did not.

### Belonging: Belonging

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: shared. Axis: Belonging

How far you feel the others want you among them: whether they make room for you and draw you in, or leave you at the edge. It is their acceptance of you, read from how they treat you, not whether they defer to you for skill or contribution (Esteem), where you stand in the order (Rank) or how they judge your conduct (Repute). The culture's face sets what the acceptance rests on. It is not your own fondness for them (Warmth).

- 10: Others make room for you on sight and draw you into whatever is going on; you never doubt that you are wanted here.
- 1: Gatherings form without you and you are left at the edge; you doubt that anyone wants you here.
- Pre-language: Being let into the huddle or kept out of it, time after time. The others shift to make a place, share, groom and turn towards her, or close ranks, turn away and leave her at the fringe, and keep doing so. Intimates read it from whether she moves freely into the middle of the group or hovers at its edge, watching for a gap.

Face in the egalitarian deck: Wanted as one of their own in good standing. The others want you among them as one of their own in good standing: you pull with the group as far as you are able and do not feed on it. The acceptance does not depend on how much you produce or on any place in an order, though being looked to for what you bring can draw you further in. Nor is it unconditional: a member the group has judged to shirk, cheat or feed on the others (Repute) is edged out. You read it from how the group as a whole treats you, not from the judgement itself.
- 10: Others make room for you and draw you in as a matter of course, and have done so through good seasons and bad; you feel it would take shirking, cheating or turning on the group to change it.
- 1: Gatherings form without you and you are left at the edge: they treat you as a freeloader or an outsider, not as one of their own.
- Pre-language: Let into the huddle after a failed hunt as readily as after a good one, so long as she still pulls with the band; the one who shirks, cheats or feeds on the others is edged out, however able.

Face in the hierarchical deck: Included while you keep your place. The others keep you among them so long as you keep to what your place in the order calls for, high or low, and do not step out of line. The acceptance is real but conditional on keeping the place, not on the place itself (Rank). You read it from how they treat you while you keep it and from the standing threat of being dropped, not from whether anyone of standing has charged you with stepping out of line or whether that charge has been answered (Face).
- 10: You have kept your place and the others keep you among them: at every gathering a place is made for you, whether high or low, as a matter of course.
- 1: You have stepped out of line and been dropped, or were never given a place at all: gatherings form without you and you are kept at the edge.
- Pre-language: Kept in the group, at whatever rank she holds, for as long as she does what her place calls for; the moment she oversteps, the others turn on her or shut her out, and she feels the edge of that long before it happens.

### Prestige: Prestige

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: shared. Axis: Respect

How far the others defer to you freely for what you bring: they turn to you, watch and copy how you do it, bring you their problems and take your advice. It is not whether they want you among them (Belonging), your rung in the settled order (Rank) or their verdict on your conduct (Repute). The culture's face sets how far the deference reaches.

- 10: Whenever your kind of skill or contribution counts, the others turn to you, watch and copy how you do it, bring you their problems and take your advice.
- 1: No one asks you, watches you or copies you; when your kind of skill or contribution is needed they turn to someone else.
- Pre-language: Being the one others watch, copy and seek out: eyes on her hands, bodies drawn near while she works, problems brought to her, her way of doing things taken up; as children attend to and learn from models whom others attend to. Intimates read it from who gets watched, copied and sought, and who is passed over when a task of her kind comes up. In apes the pull is not cleanly apart from rank: chimpanzees copy both higher-ranking and expert models (Kendal et al. 2015), and subordinates hide what they know while dominants watch (Drea and Wallen 1999).

Face in the egalitarian deck: Looked to for what you bring, kept modest. How far you feel the others turn to you freely for what you bring the band: skill, knowledge, a good eye for the venture in hand. They watch how you do it, copy you, follow your lead on the matter in hand and bring you their problems, while you stay one of them: the deference gives you no right to command, and it lasts only while what you bring does. Whether you carry it modestly is the others' verdict on your conduct (Repute), which feeds it. Being shown up as clumsy or mistaken on a matter of skill, or as falling short on your share for want of skill rather than will, lowers it; falling short for want of will is Repute's ground. It is not whether they want you among them (Belonging), not freedom from being pushed about (Parity) and not the return on your help (Reciprocity).
- 10: Whenever your kind of skill counts, the others look to you, copy how you do it, bring you their problems and follow your lead on it for the day.
- 1: No one watches, copies or follows you on anything; when your kind of skill is needed they turn to someone else.
- Pre-language: The good hunter whose way of tracking the young copy and whose call on where to go is followed for the day, while his meat is still mocked as poor.

- Decrement: When I brace and mask myself, I keep my news and my troubles to myself and sit through their talk at arm's length, and I feel no nearer to any of them for the time spent: my fondness for them falls a little.
- Explanation: People tell others about most of their emotional episodes, and sharing builds bonds between teller and listener (Rimé 2009, review). Sharing good news that is met with active, constructive interest goes with greater closeness, trust and satisfaction (Gable, Reis, Impett and Asher 2004; couples, so carrying it to group members is the model's step). Suppressing expression, as a habit and in a new setting, predicted less closeness to others and less social support in students entering college, across self, weekly and peer reports (Srivastava et al. 2009), and suppression lowers rapport within a single conversation (Butler et al. 2003). This card carries her own fondness, raised by having told; E:DivsExp->Belonging carries the others' acceptance. Where one disclosure fires both, play both small. The increment needs the others to take her news up; where they meet it with indifference or deflate it, set it nil. Keep the decrement smaller: masking keeps her from growing closer more than it pushes her away. The rise comes over the minutes of the telling and the response. Forms a loop with E:Warmth->DivsExp; the brake is a disclosure met badly.

### E:Prestige->Warmth: Passed over for her (Reinforce, Egalitarian)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the affective scientist's second-round review (5 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When the others turn to me whenever my kind of skill counts, there is no one here for me to envy, and I warm to those who look to me: my fondness for them rises a little.
- Decrement: When they turn past me to another member for my kind of skill, I feel the sting, and where she seems not to have earned it the sting turns sour and I resent her and those who turn to her: my fondness for them falls.
- Explanation: Envy comes in two forms with different experiences and aims: benign envy moves the envier to rise, malicious envy to pull the other down (van de Ven, Zeelenberg and Pieters 2009, three countries); the two are separate dispositions, with hope for success feeding the benign form and fear of failure the malicious (Lange and Crusius 2015). Whether the other's advantage seems deserved is the appraisal that separates them (van de Ven, Zeelenberg and Pieters 2012). Size the decrement by that appraisal. Where she plainly does it better, the envy is benign: I watch how she does it and want to match her, and my fondness falls only a little. Where she seems not to have earned it (favour, show, or luck the others mistake for skill), the envy is malicious and the fall is larger. The increment is small: being looked to leaves no one to envy and warms me a little to those who look to me, which rests on common sense. A close other's success threatens most when it lies in one's own domain (Tesser 1988), so scale the card to how near she is and how much the skill is the member's own. Warmth is fondness for the members at large, so scale the fall to how much of the group the rival and those who turn to her make up. In a small band where a would-be star is levelled by ribbing, the unearned case also feeds the talk carried by X:Prestige->Repute when the pack is on; this card moves only her fondness. Envy is felt within seconds of being passed over; its cooling settles over the gathering.

### E:GrpProsp->Warmth: Going well, glad of them (Reinforce, Egalitarian)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the affective scientist's second-round review (5 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When I feel surer we can pull the venture off, the work runs light, I laugh more easily with them and am glad to be in their company: my fondness for them rises a little.
- Decrement: When the venture starts to look beyond us, the gatherings turn tense and flat and their company weighs on me: my fondness for them falls a little.
- Explanation: Members of successful groups tend to like one another more than members of unsuccessful ones (Lott and Lott 1965, a review of group cohesiveness as interpersonal attraction), so a lift in the venture's prospects warms her to the members and a sag cools her. Affect tracks the rate of progress towards a goal against what was expected (Carver and Scheier 1990, searched), and in groups positive mood spreading among members raised cooperation and lowered conflict while negative mood did the reverse (Barsade 2002), a supporting route through shared mood. Performance feeds cohesion mainly through commitment to the task, with liking a smaller part (Mullen and Copper 1994, cited by Model B, not checked here), so keep the card small. Boundary: the same review finds that shared failure can also raise liking, especially where it is imposed arbitrarily from outside (Lott and Lott 1965); where the venture sags under an outside blow they all bear, set the decrement nil, and fondness may hold or rise in play. Warmth here reads the members as a whole. Fire it on a change in Team Confidence, not on a steady level: a venture long known to be in reach lifts no one. Distinct from E:GrpProsp->Belonging, which carries the others' ease with her.
