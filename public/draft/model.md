## Pillars

### GrpProsp: Group Prospects

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: shared. Axis: Prospects

How far you feel the group, as it now stands, can pull off the venture it has taken on and not yet finished: a game, project, season or campaign. It is confidence in the capability the venture will run on, not luck, not your own prospects (Payoff), not whether each will do their part (Dependability) and not a general sense that the group is strong. The culture's face sets where that capability is looked for.

- 10: You feel sure the group, as it stands, can pull off what it has set itself; the venture looks well within its reach.
- 1: You feel sure the group, as it stands, cannot pull off what it has set itself; the venture looks beyond it.
- Pre-language: A felt sense, before any words, that this band can bring off what it is setting out to do: chimpanzee males who hear strangers chorus and advance together when their party holds three or more males, and stay silent or hold back when it holds fewer (Wilson et al. 2001); and the chimpanzee who picks as partner the one who has made a joint task work before (Melis et al. 2006). Intimates read it from her bearing as the venture starts: settled and forward-leaning, in step with the others, or hesitant and watchful, eyes on the others rather than the task.

Face in the egalitarian deck: Capability spread across the members. How far you feel the group, as it now stands, can pull off the venture it has agreed to take on and not yet finished. It is confidence in the group's capability for that venture, spread across its members, their skill included, and including the skill of whoever the band looks to for this venture for the day. Whether that member lords it over you is Parity's ground, and whether the others look to you is Esteem's. It is not luck, not your own prospects (Payoff), not whether each will do their part (Dependability), and not a general sense that the group is strong.
- 10: You feel sure the group, as it stands, can pull off what it has set itself; the venture looks well within its reach.
- 1: You feel sure the group, as it stands, cannot pull off what it has set itself; the venture looks beyond it.

Face in the hierarchical deck: Confidence in those who steer. How far you feel those at the top of the order, yourself included if you are there, can steer the group through the venture it has taken on and not yet finished: read the ground, choose the course, set each member to the part that suits them and right the course when it goes wrong. It reads their competence to direct this venture only, with the hands they have: whether the skill below is fit for the venture is read here as part of their steering, so a crew they have kept on a venture beyond it lowers this reading. A shortfall they could not have avoided or remedied, such as fixed capacity or an outside shock, lowers it less than one they chose or failed to correct. It is not whether they deal evenly with you, hearing you, holding you to the same rules and serving no favourites first (Parity), not the deference you draw for what you bring (Esteem), not your rung in the order (Rank), not what staying will bring you (Payoff), and not whether those below will carry their parts (Dependability). A well-meaning leader out of their depth rates low; a hard one who knows the way rates high.
- 10: You feel sure those at the top know the way through what the group has taken on; you follow their lead without second-guessing it, and if you are among them you feel the course is in safe hands.
- 1: You feel sure those at the top cannot steer the group through what it has taken on; each call they make looks like the wrong one, and if you are among them you feel the course slipping out of your hands.
- Pre-language: A felt sense, before any words, that the ones in front know where to take the band: the troop's readiness to rise and follow when the lead animal moves off, as subordinate chacma baboons followed the dominant male's foraging moves even at a cost to themselves (King et al. 2008), against hanging back, lagging or drifting off when the lead's moves have come to nothing. Read with care: in King's study following depended on the followers' social bonds with the leader, so it rests on ties as well as on any reading of where he leads; and in olive baboons movement is settled among many initiators and the dominant is not simply followed (Strandburg-Peshkin et al. 2015). Following a single leader is therefore one primate pattern among several, and the model borrows it for ranked orders only. Intimates read it from how she meets a call from the top: moving with it at once, eyes on the task, or a pause, a glance at the others and a slow, half-hearted start; and, if she is at the front herself, from a sure, unhurried move off against a false start and a look back.

Effect on Engagement: That is likely to succeed in goals that you believe are worth pursing, or you would not have joined the group in the first place, and that has agreed goals, or otherwise random changes to the group's direction will move outside the model's ability to predict.

### OwnProsp: Own Prospects

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: HCP. Axis: Prospects

How likely you feel it is that staying in this group will get you what you yourself are after from it: the place, play, pay, skill, advancement or mate you joined for. It is a forecast of what the group will deliver to you, not your own ability, not your present rung in the order (Rank), not the deference you draw now for what you bring (Esteem), not whether the others want you among them now (Belonging), not the group's own venture (Team Confidence), not whether help you give comes back (Reciprocity), not whether anyone takes more than their due or tilts a share-out against you now (Parity), and not a tally of what it has given so far. Each of these can feed the forecast without being it.

- 10: You are sure that staying will bring you what you came for; the group's dealings with you keep opening the way to it.
- 1: You are sure that staying will bring you nothing you came for; whatever you do here, the way to it stays shut.
- Pre-language: The felt yield of staying with this band for her: whether being here keeps bringing her food, a mate, a place or a skill, or keeps bringing her nothing. Intimates read it from whether she settles in and works at her own aims here, or drifts to the edge, looks outward and leaves.

Effect on Engagement: Where you feel that you are likely to achieve your personal goals, whether or not they are part of the group goals (Team Confidence) or indeed, conflict with them. Presumably if personal and group goals are in conflict, you would prefer to have low levels of Team Confidence however such a condition is out of scope for the model as it indicates deception and potentially sabotage, which is a cognitive rather than an unconscious drive (as the Pillars are contended to be).

### Status: Status

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: HCP. Axis: Standing

Your felt rung in the group's settled order of precedence: how far you go first, take the first turn and the first share as a matter of course, and how far you go after others and take what is left. All recognise the order, so it is not fought out each time. A single call taken or refused moves it little; it shifts as such acts settle into what all take for granted. The precedence attaches to your place, however you came by it: an office, seniority, the backing of kin or allies, an old contest no one reopens, or credit for what you bring that has settled, over time, into a place others take for granted. It is distinct from the live deference you draw for what you bring now (Esteem), and once settled it does not need present fear to hold it day to day (Parity), though an old contest or the backing of allies may stand behind it.

- 10: You go first as a matter of course: the first turn, the first share and the best seat are yours without a word said, and no one in the group stands above you.
- 1: You go last as a matter of course: you wait your turn and take your share and seat after everyone else, without a word said, and no one in the group stands below you.
- Pre-language: Her felt rung in an order all recognise: the others let her pass, eat and settle first and greet her with the calm signals of submission, or she passes, eats and settles after them and greets them so, and neither side has to fight it out each time. Intimates read it from who passes first when paths cross, who eats first, who takes the best spot, and who greets whom.

Effect on Engagement: Where you feel that you possess formal power within a group, enabling you to utilise the group to achieve your own vision, which aligns the group with your identity. This is why new leaders often revoke measures taken by the preceding.

### NormExp: Normative Expression (norm guarding)

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: HCP. Axis: Expression

How strongly it strikes you as wrong when another member breaks from the group's settled ways in a way the group would condemn, in act or by openly pressing to change them. The settled ways are read from what members actually do as well as from what they say should be done: where most members have stopped keeping a way, a breach of it jars less, however firmly the way is still spoken of; where most keep it, a lone breach jars most. That softening holds for the group's conventions, how things are done here. A breach that wrongs a member, taking from her, hurting her or leaving her to carry what was the breacher's to carry, jars whatever most members now do and whoever commits it. Your reading can still run stricter than what the others privately hold, since members often take a way that is openly kept to be more widely endorsed than it privately is (pluralistic ignorance: Prentice and Miller 1993). A sense of being out of step with the others can follow from such a misreading, and in that study it went with alienation from the university; it is not part of this reading, and it bears on Belonging only through how you then read the others' treatment of you. The reading starts from the breach, as felt indignation, not from any prior dislike of the person. Intimates read it from the start the breach gives you, the muttering or the open word, and not only from whether you pull the member up. An objection, a rebuke or mockery are anger's forms: they look for the member to mend their ways and be taken back. Where the breach repeats or reads as bad character, the indignation hardens into contempt for the member, and a cold shoulder or shutting them out are its forms, and they last. The reading's level does not set the form; the form sets how long the response bites and whether it ends in reconciliation or exclusion. Whether the bristle comes out as an open rebuke, a word behind the member's back or nothing at all depends on your power and on what you stand to lose with the member (Molho et al. 2020); a low member who bristles and keeps quiet still reads high. Your own conformity and your openness to ideas are not part of it. Among equals the group's ways include its levelling ways: not presuming on success or boasting, not keeping back what should be shared, and pulling your weight; a breach of these stirs the same bristle, which among equals most often comes out as ribbing and complaint (Boehm 1999; Wiessner 2005), though here too the form depends on your power and what you stand to lose. Whether such a member lords it over you yourself is Parity's ground. Whether their holding back leaves your own need unmet is Reciprocity's, and whether you can count on them to do their part is Dependability's; the bristle here answers the breach, whoever it wrongs.

- 10: The smallest departure the group would condemn jars you at once as plainly wrong, and intimates see it in you: a start, a muttered word or an open one, a mocking laugh, or a cold shoulder where the breach repeats or reads as bad character.
- 1: A member breaking the group's settled ways, or openly pressing to change them, leaves you unmoved even where the group would condemn it; nothing in you says it is wrong, and intimates see no start, no mutter and no word as you let it pass.
- Pre-language: The bristle at one of the band doing a thing the wrong way. Its sign is the move to pull them up: a protest cry, a shove, a cold shoulder or exclusion. In one small theft experiment, captive chimpanzees retaliated for their own losses but did not punish theft from others (Riedl et al. 2012); the era rests on that one study and is provisional. Intimates read it from how she meets another's departure: with indignation, shown in a start, a mutter or a rebuke, or with a shrug. Forager bands such as the Ju/'hoansi turn a like bristle, by criticism and mockery, on upstarts and stingy members (Wiessner 2005); in this model that egalitarian form is carried by Safety->Parity and stands here only as the contrast. The face here is the guarding of a ranked group's settled ways.

Effect on Engagement: Where you feel that you can manage (minimise unwelcome or surprising) change, by using your power to reduce any propensity for others to suggest change. While high Rule-Keeping undermines Psychological Safety for others, it makes you personally feel safer.

### OutResp: Outgoing Respect

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: ECP. Axis: Respect

How far you feel the other members can be counted on to do their part in the shared work: each carries through what they take on and keeps their word about it, so you leave work in their hands without checking that they are doing it. The reliance may rest on their own will or on knowing that a part let drop gets pulled up by the group or its rules; either can raise it. It is reliability, not skill (Team Confidence), not whether a slip or weak spot you show would be used against you (Safety), and not your indignation at a member who lets a part drop (Rule-Keeping); that answers the breach, while this reads what you expect of them next. A part let drop likely moves it further than a part carried through. Parts you have seen carried through or dropped move it directly and outweigh any card that reads it from your own fondness, confidence in the group, acceptance or investment; those cards bite only on parts not yet done or whose outcome you cannot see.

- 10: You leave any part of the work with any member and never check whether they are doing it; what they take on gets done and what they promise about it holds.
- 1: You check up on and chase others' work and redo what they skimp, expecting them to shirk, cut corners or go back on their word.
- Pre-language: Whether she can turn her back on them at a shared task. One who counts on her fellows leaves the watch, the carry or the young in their hands and goes about her own business without looking back; one who does not keeps glancing over, hovers at their work or takes it over herself. Intimates read it from how often she looks back, checks and takes over. The mechanism is trust calibrated to a partner's record of carrying a joint task, the kind of partner assessment the interdependence account places early in human cooperation (Tomasello et al. 2012). What sets this Pillar apart from Reciprocity is what the record is of: the partner's share of a joint task, not a favour returned to her. It is her reading of them; whether they turn their backs on her is a different thing and not read here.

Effect on Engagement: With competent and trustworthy people because you like being associated with 'winners', you admire and wish to model yourself off them, and they can help you to improve.

### Levelling: Levelling

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: shared. Axis: Standing

How far you feel no one in the group sets themselves over you unfairly: by force or fear, by taking more than their due and keeping it, by tilting how shares, turns and chances are handed out, who gets a hearing, or how a quarrel is settled, towards themselves or their favourites, or by treating you with contempt, as if above you. The culture's face sets where that threat is looked for: among equals, a would-be boss; in a ranked group, partial or overbearing dealing from those above.

- 10: No one in the group makes you comply by force or threat, takes more than their due and keeps it, or tilts a share-out, a hearing or the settling of a quarrel against you; you take your portion and go about your business without watching anyone warily.
- 1: One or a few in the group make you comply through force or fear, take more than their due and keep it, or decide and divide in favour of their own while you are passed over or go unheard; you watch them warily or resent them, and mostly let them have their way, because objecting gets you nowhere or draws their anger.
- Pre-language: Freedom from a bully she must appease. The free animal moves, feeds and takes her share in the strongest member's presence without flinching or a glance at that member's mood; the coerced one tracks that member's moods, freezes on approach and does whatever heads off an attack. Intimates read it from whether she watches one particular member warily and shrinks or placates when that member comes near, while staying easy with everyone else, and whether, at a share-out, she is handed her portion in turn or watches the one dividing it feed his friends first and pass her by. The root is dominance, the coalitions that check it and the option to leave (Boehm 1999), not primate inequity aversion.

Face in the egalitarian deck: No one lords it over you. How far you feel no one in the group sets themselves over you: makes you do, drop or endure things by force or fear; takes more than the group's ordinary share-out gives them, of food, credit or say, and makes it stick; tilts how shares, turns and chances are handed out, or how a quarrel is settled, towards themselves or their favourites, passing you over; or treats you with contempt, as if above you. You go along of your own accord, unafraid, not shortchanged and not passed over. The reaction that lowers it takes one of two forms, depending on whether you could answer the wrong. Alone against force, it is wariness and fear, and you keep away or appease. With others who would back you, or where the wrong is a grab or a tilted share-out rather than a threat, it is aggrieved anger, and you want to confront them. Either form lowers the reading by the same amount; they differ in what you then do and in which cards bite. Where you could move to another group at little cost, a would-be boss has less hold over you, and the same push lowers this reading less; where leaving is costly, it lowers it more. It is not rank (Rank), acceptance (Belonging), deference for what you bring (Esteem), the return on help or whether those with a surplus of their own share it on demand (Reciprocity), whether others do their part of the work (Dependability), your indignation at a breach of the group's ways whoever it wrongs (Rule-Keeping), the cost of showing yourself (Safety), the others' verdict on your own conduct (Repute) or whether a charge against your character stands before those who count (Face). A respected leader, a settled order you accept, a share-out that leaves you less this time by a way of dividing you can see is even-handed (equal chances, turns in order, or a say in how it is done), or the group together pulling up a breach of its ways leaves it high; a member you must appease, one who grabs and gets away with it, or one who hands out or settles in favour of their own lowers it. A share-out that leaves you plainly less than a peer, with no even-handed way of dividing in view, lowers it a little even where no one grabbed or tilted it; the fall is smaller than for a grab or a tilt, and is made good once an even-handed way is shown. Where the group has spoken ways of deciding, having those ways applied to you as to anyone, and a fair hearing for your say, feed the same reading even when the outcome goes against you; a say that is heard and then brushed aside, never weighed, does not raise it.
- 10: No member or clique can make you do or drop anything by force or threat, no one takes more than their due and keeps it, and whoever hands out or settles things does so with no thumb on the scale. You move, take your share and go about your business in the strongest member's presence as freely as in anyone's.
- 1: One or a few members make you comply through force or fear, take more than their due and keep it, or hand out and settle things in favour of their own while you are passed over. Alone against them, you watch them warily, fall quiet when they come near, and let them have their way to head off their anger. Where others would back you, or the wrong is a grab or a tilt rather than a threat, it galls you and you want to confront them, though you may still let it pass because objecting gets you nowhere.
- Pre-language: Freedom from a bully she must appease. The free animal moves, feeds and takes her share in the strongest member's presence without flinching or a glance at that member's mood; the coerced one tracks that member's moods, freezes on approach and does whatever heads off an attack. Intimates read it from whether she watches one particular member warily and shrinks or placates when that member comes near, while staying easy with everyone else, and whether, at a share-out, she is handed her portion in turn or watches the one dividing it feed his friends first and pass her by.

Face in the hierarchical deck: Even hands from those above. How far you feel those above you in the order, and the order's own ways of deciding, deal evenly with you: they hear you before deciding what touches you, hold you to the same rules as anyone at your rung, hand out shares, turns and chances without serving their favourites first, settle quarrels without a thumb on the scale, give orders only as far as the rules of your place allow, without threat or fear, and treat you with the courtesy your place is owed. It is not your rung (Rank), not whether partners repay you (Reciprocity), not whether those at the top can steer (Team Confidence), not deference for what you bring (Esteem), not the cost of showing yourself (Safety), not whether you are kept among them (Belonging) and not whether a charge against your character stands before those who count (Face), though being denied a hearing on such a charge lowers this reading. A settled order that gives you less by rank but applies its rules evenly and hears you leaves it high; a decision made over your head, a patron who serves his own first, a superior who rules by threat, contempt from above, or one above who lets a wrong done to you by another member stand when it is theirs to put right, lowers it.
- 10: Those above you hear you out before any call that touches you, apply the same rules to you as to anyone at your rung and hand out turns and shares with no favourite served first; you take your portion when it comes without watching the one who divides it.
- 1: Calls that touch you are made over your head, favourites are served before you whatever the rules say, and objecting gets you waved off or met with a threat; you watch whoever divides, settles and gives orders with a wary, aggrieved eye.
- Pre-language: Whether the one who divides and settles hands her portion in its turn and hears her out, or feeds his favourites first and waves her off. Intimates read it from whether she waits at the share-out at ease or watches the divider warily, and whether she puts her case to those above or no longer bothers. After a clash with one above her, a soft grunt or easy approach from him tells her it is over and she stays near; a hard stare sends her off. When those above break up fights without taking sides, she feeds and rests among them at ease.

### DivsExp: Diverse Expression (felt safety)

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: ECP. Axis: Expression

How safe you feel to show yourself in this group: to let a slip, a mood, a need or a disagreement show, or go about things your own way, expecting no put-down, mockery or rebuff for it. It includes whether a slip, a need or a weak spot you let show would be turned against you to get ahead of you or to do you harm. It is your reading of what showing yourself will cost you, not whether you speak up; not whether a need you show gets met (Reciprocity), whether your word carries weight (Esteem) or whether you are wanted (Belonging); not whether anyone pushes you about by force or fear, whether those who decide hear you out before a call that touches you or whether anyone treats you with contempt, as if above you, whatever you show (Parity); not the verdict already held on your conduct (Repute) or being openly charged or caught out before those who count (Face); and not your own indignation at others' departures (Rule-Keeping).

- 10: You feel free to let slips, moods, needs and disagreements show as they come and to go about things your own way, expecting no one to pounce on them, laugh at them or turn away.
- 1: You feel that letting a slip, a mood, a plain need, a disagreement or a way of your own show would draw a put-down or be used against you, so you scan faces before you move and keep your reactions masked.
- Pre-language: Relaxed against braced among her own: the loose body and open play of an animal that expects no bite from its fellows, against the scanning, tense stillness of one that does. Intimates read it from how loose or tight she is when eyes turn to her, and from whether she hides a hurt or a want.

Effect on Engagement: Where you feel that your ideas will be welcomed and well-received. While related to IncResp (HCP), Expression is closer to a norm of Psychological Safety than being given specific respect for one's competence. Such a norm creates a warm, less hostile environment, which is distinct from cohesion: a close, friendly group can still make challenge feel risky.

### Reciprocity: Reciprocity

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: shared. Axis: Reciprocity

Your felt reading of whether help in this group comes back: what you give is returned, what you ask for is met. It holds until evidence moves it. A return that falls short of what was given or agreed moves it further, and is slower to be made good, than a return that exceeds it. It concerns the return on help, not whether others carry their part of shared work (Dependability), whether anyone takes more of a common take than their due (Parity), your indignation at a member who keeps back what should be shared (Rule-Keeping), where you stand (Rank), whether the others want you among them (Belonging) or how they judge your conduct (Repute, Face), though your good name feeds the return. The culture's face sets where the return is looked for.

- 10: What you give comes back and what you ask for is met, as a matter of course; you count on the return without a second thought.
- 1: What you give is not returned and what you need goes unmet; you expect nothing back from anyone here.
- Pre-language: The felt return on sharing: grooming that is returned by those groomed (Schino and Aureli 2008), and forager food handed over on demand to whoever asks (Peterson 1993), with those who have given tending to be given to in turn (Allen-Arave, Gurven and Hill 2008). Intimates read it as a sign in her behaviour: whether she hands over on request and asks as of right, expecting the return, or hesitates, hoards and goes without rather than ask.

Face in the egalitarian deck: Return from the group. Help goes to whoever needs it, and the return comes from the group at large. Big windfalls often go out by demand and tolerated taking, to whoever is there, whatever their record, though that is one route among several. In some bands the care a member gets when sick or hurt, and some everyday giving, track being seen to share; in others even everyday giving goes by need, whatever the record. Whoever has a surplus is pressed to share on demand, and asking is as normal as giving. Your reading is whether, when you are in need, the group at large gives to you.
- 10: Whoever has gives to whoever needs; what you have put in comes back to you from any member when you are in need.
- 1: Giving goes into a hole: nothing comes back from anyone, those with a surplus hide it, and need goes unmet.
- Pre-language: Food handed over on demand within the band, with the return coming from whoever next has a surplus; those who hide food lose standing and are passed over. Intimates read it as a sign in her behaviour: whether she hands her surplus to anyone who asks and asks anyone who has, or holds back from both.

Face in the hierarchical deck: Return from the partner. Help flows between particular partners who return it to each other. It is conditional on a return from the same partner: in kind between equals, or across rank, as favour and protection passed down for loyalty and service passed up. Your reading is whether your partners pay you back, not where you stand (Rank), whether they do their part of the shared work (Dependability), whether those above decide evenly (Parity) or whether your character stands unchallenged before those who count (Face), though being shown up as one who leaves a favour unreturned lowers Face, and Face in turn feeds the return.
- 10: Every partner you help pays you back and those you ask deliver; whoever stands above you covers you and whoever stands below you backs you, each in turn.
- 1: Partners take and do not return; whoever stands above you leaves you uncovered and whoever stands below you will not back you; you expect no return from anyone you deal with.
- Pre-language: Grooming returned by the one groomed (Schino and Aureli 2008). Intimates read it as a sign in her behaviour: whether she keeps giving to those who gave to her and turns from those who did not.

### Belonging: Belonging

Status: Tightened by the literature 3 Nov 2026 (docs/model-edits/tighten/); provisional

Continuum: shared. Axis: Belonging

How far you feel the others want you among them: whether they make room for you and draw you in, or leave you at the edge. It is their acceptance of you, read from how they treat you, not whether they defer to you for skill or contribution (Esteem), where you stand in the order (Rank), how they judge your conduct (Repute) or whether shares, turns and hearings come to you evenly (Parity). Being left out in a way you accept as the group's ordinary practice (a meeting for another role, a turn not yours) moves it less than being left out where you would expect to be drawn in. The culture's face sets what the acceptance rests on. It is not your own fondness for them (Warmth). Having one or more mutual close friends among them is a different thing and is not read here; it is the scene state Close tie.

- 10: Others make room for you on sight and draw you into whatever is going on; you never doubt that you are wanted here.
- 1: Gatherings form without you and you are left at the edge; you doubt that anyone wants you here.
- Pre-language: Being let into the huddle or kept out of it, time after time. The others shift to make a place, share, groom and turn towards her, or close ranks, turn away and leave her at the fringe, and keep doing so. Intimates read it from whether she moves freely into the middle of the group or hovers at its edge, watching for a gap.

Face in the egalitarian deck: Wanted as one of their own in good standing. The others want you among them as one of their own in good standing: you pull with the group as far as you are able and do not feed on it. The acceptance does not depend on how much you produce or on any place in an order, though being looked to for what you bring can draw you further in. Nor is it unconditional: a member the group has judged to shirk, cheat or feed on the others (Repute) tends to be edged out. You read it from how the group as a whole treats you, not from the judgement itself.
- 10: Others make room for you and draw you in as a matter of course, and have done so through good seasons and bad; you feel it would take shirking, cheating or turning on the group to change it.
- 1: Gatherings form without you and you are left at the edge: they treat you as a freeloader or an outsider, not as one of their own.
- Pre-language: Let into the huddle after a failed hunt as readily as after a good one, so long as she still pulls with the band; the one who shirks, cheats or feeds on the others tends to be edged out, however able.

Face in the hierarchical deck: Included while you keep your place. The reading stays on Esteem's ground; deference owed to the place itself is Rank and is not carried here. A superior who takes her credit, or decides without hearing her, is Parity's ground (H:Parity->Esteem, and I:Parity->Esteem#2 in the scene) and is not carried here either. With H:Esteem->Rank it forms a reinforcing pair. The brake is Esteem evidence, a visible failure of what she brings. Boundary, provisional: the halo is strongest where a contribution's worth is hard to judge and credit is assigned opaquely, and weakens where quality is easily seen or credit is transparent, since a plain result can be seen whoever offers it (consistent with Huber et al. 2022, searched, though that work does not test this boundary). The increment rests on that unchecked expectation, so keep it modest. The holding back is the same act that lowers Safety on X:Rank->Safety; do not count one act on both in a scene. Where the scene stages the others crediting or passing over what she puts forward, play the interaction card I:Rank->Esteem instead of this one, never both.

### H:Prestige->Status: Settling into a place (Reinforce, Hierarchical)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the comparative psychologist's critique of VX (5 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When the others keep turning to me for what I bring, leave the first word to me, hand me decisions and leave jobs that matter in my hands, I come to take that first word as mine as a matter of course, and I feel it has become my place: my felt rung in the order rises.
- Decrement: When what I bring stops counting and my word is doubted in front of the others, with nothing that matters handed to me, I stop expecting the first word and give way: my felt rung in the order slips a little.
- Explanation: Freely conferred deference is a route to rank (Henrich and Gil-White; Cheng et al.). Contributors are repaid with standing and influence (Willer), and perceived competence buys influence (Anderson and Kilduff). Once established, status holds itself through expectations (Magee and Galinsky). Those works describe the others conferring rank, which belongs to the scene; here she settles into the place, or lets it go, herself as the deference she reads builds up, a step resting on common sense. Respect for skill need not become rank or influence (von Rueden, Gurven and Kaplan 2008), so the increment rests on deference that shows up as decisions and jobs that matter handed to her; esteem alone, with nothing handed over, does not fire it. The rise needs the others to leave the first word to her; deference alone, with no turn ceded, moves Rank little. Across 276 samples, status in face-to-face groups fitted a micropolitics path best and competence and virtue paths only tentatively (Grosz et al. 2024, as reported by the reviewer, not checked here), as the I twin's boundary already notes. The step from live deference to a place taken for granted is slow. Proposal: each play moves Rank by a small fraction of Esteem's level, so the settling builds over many plays; make the decrement weaker than the increment, because a settled place outlasts the skill that earned it. That asymmetry rests on theory only (Magee and Galinsky 2008; expectation-states accounts of how a standing order preserves itself) and has no direct test. It applies once a place is established: in a new group's early weeks, places still move when contributions fall short, so there play the decrement at full strength. This card absorbs the older model's most-used card: being trusted with something that matters is Esteem evidence, and its settling is this card.

### H:Belonging->Status: Backed into my place (Reinforce, Hierarchical)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the comparative psychologist's critique of VX (5 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When I am kept among them and a place is made for me as a matter of course, and a turn or a share of mine is questioned, I feel the room is with me and hold to it without a word: my felt rung in the order, shaken by the question, firms up again.
- Decrement: When I am kept at the edge and my turn or my share is questioned in front of the others, I feel no one here would speak for me, and I give way: my felt rung in the order falls a little.
- Explanation: In macaques, kin support matters for winning rank (Chapais and Larose 1988), though keeping rank among peers need not depend on it when subordinates cannot form coalitions (same source), and grooming is traded for agonistic support (Schino 2007), in line with Seyfarth's (1977) model of grooming directed up the hierarchy for rank-related benefits. Those findings concern support from particular partners in return for service. That is the ground of H Reciprocity, where a patron covers you. This card reads the acceptance of the group as a whole and takes from the primate work, by analogy only, that whether a challenge to a place succeeds depends partly on who backs it. The card therefore fires only when a turn or share of hers is contested; it does not carry a rank held steady by acceptance alone. The human step has no direct test and is a proposal, common sense in any committee: a member kept at the edge whose turn is questioned feels no one would speak for her and gives way, while one kept among them feels the room with her and holds. The decrement's route through felt self-worth is uncertain: two 2009 meta-analyses of rejection disagree on whether exclusion lowers self-esteem (Blackhart et al. 2009; Gerber and Wheeler 2009), so the card leans on her reading that no one would back her more than on any drop in her worth. Whether the questioner in fact yields belongs to the scene. Hierarchical acceptance is held at any rung, high or low. The increment therefore only firms up a turn or share that was in doubt and never lifts her above her place; keep it smaller than the decrement. It is distinct from Esteem: the felt assent is for who she is to them, not for what she brings. Where the questioning is an open charge, by someone of standing, that she has stepped out of her place, the charge itself is Face's ground and reaches Rank through H:Face->Rank; this card then carries only whether the room backs her, and the drop in Rank is counted once in the scene.

### E:Belonging->OutResp: Friends get the rope (Reinforce, Egalitarian)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the comparative psychologist's critique of VX (5 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When the others draw me in as one of their own, I expect them not to let one of their own down on the work we share, so I hand them parts of it without a second thought and leave them to it: my confidence that they will do their part rises.
- Decrement: When I am left at the edge, I expect them to feel no call to keep their word to someone who is not one of their own, so I keep the work in my own hands and check what little I hand over: my confidence that they will do their part falls.
- Explanation: The felt shift at the cue rests on group-based trust: people extend more trust to fellow members of their own group because they expect them to treat members well (Foddy, Platow and Yamagishi 2009, not checked here). The ingroup effect in trust games is small (Romano et al. 2017; the size of about d 0.14 is not checked here) and appears to depend on reputational stakes, on membership being common knowledge and on culture (moderators reported in the meta-analysis of Balliet, Wu and De Dreu 2014 as well; neither source checked here for this card, including the claim that it is weaker in Japan). That work compares ingroup with outgroup; carrying it to graded acceptance within one group is the model's extension, and so is narrowing expected good treatment to expecting members not to let one of their own down on shared work. Being drawn in as one of their own tells her she is a member they will not leave in the lurch, so she leaves work in their hands more readily. Whether help she gives or asks for comes back is Reciprocity's ground and is not carried here. The decrement, that a member left at the edge trusts less and checks more, has no direct test and rests on the same extension. Parts she has seen carried through or dropped outweigh this card, as Dependability's definition says, so it bites only on parts not yet done or whose outcome she cannot see. Trust that rests on her own fondness for the members is carried by E:Warmth->Dependability; where both fire, play this card small. The gain for Dependability also builds across plays from sampling, a common-sense step pending a check of Fetchenhauer and Dunning 2010 and Denrell 2005: a member who trusts more sees more work carried through, while one who withholds trust sees little and has nothing to correct her last let-down. Control read as distrust lowers the other's effort (Falk and Kosfeld 2006), an effect that is real but usually small on replication (Ziegelmeyer, Schmelz and Ploner 2012); that is the others' response and belongs to the scene, so over many plays withheld trust may prove partly self-fulfilling there, not on this card. Keep the card modest.

### H:Reciprocity->Prestige: Generous, so sought (Reinforce, Hierarchical)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the comparative psychologist's critique of VX (5 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When the partners I help pay me back as a matter of course, I can afford to give freely, beyond any one partner and in the others' sight, and I feel myself one with help worth bringing their problems to: my sense that they turn to me for what I bring rises.
- Decrement: When the partners I help do not pay me back, I stop giving beyond what I must, and I feel I have less in hand that the others would come to me for: my sense that they turn to me for what I bring falls a little.
- Explanation: In public settings, the most generous members gain the most status, here freely given deference (Esteem), and are the most preferred partners (Hardy and Van Vugt 2006), and contributors are given influence and deference (Willer 2009). Both studies concern contributions to a common pool; the step to help given beyond her own partners in the others' sight is by analogy. Repayment only sustains the giving: a felt return makes open-handed giving affordable to her, a step resting on common sense and untested, and her felt esteem comes from the open-handed giving itself, never from being repaid. Work on helping and status ties esteem to giving more than one receives (Flynn et al. 2006). The card carries her felt reading as that giving opens or closes, a cautious proxy for what the others confer; their answering deference belongs to the scene (rulebook 8.5). The standing gain holds for visible, open-handed giving and fades when the giving reads as strategic or as a paid trade (Bliege Bird and Power 2015; a further source cited earlier as Livingston, Cohen and Halevy 2010 is not confirmed and needs checking, the nearest found being Halevy, Chou, Cohen and Livingston 2012). Each play moves her felt reading a step; the deference builds over many plays. Her name as a sharer is Repute's ground and is not carried here. The card forms a positive loop with H:Prestige->Reciprocity; the brake is a partner who stops repaying.

### X:Status->Levelling: Low rung, easy target (Reinforce, X)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the comparative psychologist's critique of VX (5 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When I hold a high place in a settled order, I feel no one would try to push me about, take my due or pass me over when things are handed out, and I go about my business beside the strongest without a glance at their mood: my sense that no one here can force me, take more than their due or hand out unfairly, and get away with it, rises.
- Decrement: When I sit low in an order I have not accepted, the one who likes to push is about and no one here reins him in, I read myself as his easy target, open to losing my turns and my share beyond what the order gives him and to being passed over when he hands things out to his own; I start to watch him and do what heads off his temper: my sense that no one here can force me, take more than their due or hand out unfairly, and get away with it, falls.
- Explanation: Rank shapes how much aggression and stress an animal receives, but direction and size depend on the society: subordination is most stressful in stable despotic orders and not in egalitarian ones, and in unstable orders the high-ranked face the most challenges (Sapolsky 2005); formalised, reconciled orders damp overt aggression (de Waal 1986). In a settled order she accepts, a low place moves Parity little, and an even-handed share-out that gives her less by rank leaves it high, as the Parity definition says; a low place in a settled, legitimate order may not lower the reading at all and can raise tolerance of unequal shares (Albrecht et al. 2012, not confirmed; needs checking, and the step rests on the Parity definition and common sense meanwhile). In well-levelled groups low rank carries less exposure (Boehm 1993). The card bites only when someone who could use her low place to push, take or pass her over is about and the group does little to check him; the decrement is her reading of her own exposure to him. In peer-victimisation studies the link runs both ways over time, low standing among peers predicting later victimisation and victimisation predicting later low standing (Hodges and Perry 1999, children; not checked here); the standing there was peer rejection, nearer Belonging than Rank, so it is adjacent support only, and the card claims no more than her reading of exposure. His actual grabs belong to the scene. Set it small in settled orders and larger in despotic ones. While places are being contested, set the increment to zero, since a high place then draws challenges instead of shielding her. Where she has accepted the order she sits low in, play the decrement nil. Limb: coercion, grab, allocation.

### E:DivsExp->Prestige: Ideas aired, ideas taken up (Reinforce, Egalitarian)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the social psychologist's critique of VX (5 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When I feel safe to let a half-formed idea show, I put forward how we might do the work better in front of the others, and I feel I have shown them something worth turning to me for: my sense that they turn to me for what I bring rises a little.
- Decrement: When I brace and keep my ideas masked, what I could bring to the work in hand stays in my head, and I feel that on this matter the others have seen nothing of mine to turn to me for: my sense that they turn to me for what I bring falls a little.
- Explanation: Felt psychological safety is taken as a precondition for letting half-formed ideas show, since an untested idea risks a slip or a disagreement shown, which is Safety's ground; the link from safety to voice is carried by Safety's evidence and is not rechecked here. The card now carries only her own felt sense of having shown, or withheld, something worth turning to her for, which rests on common sense; play it small. The evidence on status conferred for speaking up measures the others' response and belongs to the scene: speaking up raises the status colleagues confer, partly because those who speak are seen as more agentic (Weiss and Morrison 2019); promotive voice raised peer-rated status and later leader emergence, while prohibitive voice did not, and men gained from promotive voice and women did not (McClean et al. 2018); members high in trait dominance are rated more competent by peers (Anderson and Kilduff 2009). The others' taking up of what she airs is I:DivsExp->Prestige (Idea aired, idea taken up); do not count it again here. The card covers promotive voice only, ideas offered to improve the shared work; voicing alarm or criticism is not covered. Where she expects a status penalty for speaking up, as McClean et al. found for women, the felt gain is smaller or absent. The gain tracks visible contribution more than its quality (Anderson and Kilduff 2009), so the card reads that she has shown something, whatever its worth. The decrement reads only the matter in hand: deference she already draws for a skill the others have seen is not lowered by an idea kept back. Deck E: the card reads Esteem's egalitarian face, deference freely given on the matter in hand with no right to command. It forms a Reinforce loop with E:Prestige->DivsExp; the brake is a visible failure of what she puts forward (Esteem evidence).

### E:OutResp->Levelling: No passengers (Reinforce, Egalitarian)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the social psychologist's critique of VX (5 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When every member carries through what they take on, the credit and reward each draws is earned by a part they have done, so no one gets more than our reckoning gives by coasting: my sense that no one here takes more than their due and keeps it holds or rises a little.
- Decrement: When some members coast yet still draw the full share of credit and reward that our reckoning gives only to those who do their part, and no one makes them give it up, I see them taking more than their due and keeping it: my sense that no one here takes more than their due and keeps it falls.
- Explanation: A coaster who draws the full share where the group's reckoning ties shares to doing one's part takes more than that reckoning gives, which is what Parity's second limb reads; the reading falls only when no one stops him and the grab sticks. The reckoning itself is early and specific to joint work: three-year-olds divide the spoils equally after collaborating but not after a windfall (Hamann et al. 2011), give a free rider less than a collaborator (Melis, Altrichter and Tomasello 2013), and keep less for themselves when they contributed less (Kanngiesser and Warneken 2012); 21-month-olds expect a reward for each who worked and not for one who did not (Sloane, Baillargeon and Premack 2012). So in shared work a member expects shares to track parts, and a coaster on a full share breaks that expectation. That breaking it lowers her felt Parity, rather than only stirring indignation, is still the model's step and rests on common sense. In public goods games cooperators punish free riders even where punishing cannot narrow the payoff gap, so their motive reads as retaliation for unfair conduct rather than inequity (Falk, Fehr and Fischbacher 2005); that anger is Rule-Keeping's ground, since pulling your weight is one of the levelling ways it guards, and X:OutResp->NormExp carries only the sensitising it leaves. Unearned pay gaps do lower output and attendance (Breza, Kaur and Shamdasani 2018), but those gaps were set by an employer, which is Parity's H face, not a coasting peer. So the card holds only where the work was joint and the group's reckoning plainly ties shares to parts, and does not fire where the band answers the coasting with ribbing and complaint as a breach of its ways, since then the grab has not stuck; one coasting scene is counted once, on this card and not also as a bare unequal share on Parity's face. Members also cut their own effort rather than carry a partner who free rides (the sucker effect: Kerr 1983), which does not move here. Where the group's reckoning gives equal shares whatever each puts in, the coaster gets what the reckoning gives and Parity is untouched; where reward tracks each member's own output, shirking costs only the shirker. High reliability removes coasting as one route to an unearned share; it says nothing of grabbing or coercion by other means. The card does not scale by a close tie. Modest; forms a Reinforce loop with E:Levelling->OutResp.

### E:Prestige->DivsExp: Credit to spend (Reinforce, Egalitarian)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the social psychologist's critique of VX (5 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When the others turn to me for what I bring and take my advice, I feel a slip, a doubt or a disagreement of mine would be shrugged off as a bad day rather than met with a put-down or a laugh: my felt safety to show myself rises.
- Decrement: When no one turns to me for anything I bring, I feel I have nothing to set against a slip, and that a doubt or a disagreement of mine would sooner draw a put-down: my felt safety to show myself falls a little.
- Explanation: Higher professional status goes with greater felt psychological safety (Nembhard and Edmondson 2006), and power releases approach and expression, while low power primes attention to threat and inhibition (Keltner, Gruenfeld and Anderson 2003). Both bear on settled place and power, which X:Status->DivsExp carries. The step to live deference is a proposal resting on common sense: a member the others turn to has something to set against a slip. Hollander's idiosyncrasy credit says members who have earned standing are allowed more latitude, but direct tests are few and weak, so it is not relied on. Work on deviance credit (Abrams et al. 2018) is a candidate neighbour once its full text confirms what it measures. The pratfall effect points the same way for a slip off the skill: a blunder made a highly competent person more liked (Aronson, Willerman and Floyd 1966), though later studies found it depends on the observer's self-esteem and sex (Helmreich, Aronson and LeFan 1970; Deaux 1972) and it measured liking, not felt safety, so it is not relied on. Standing can also raise the stakes of a slip: people react more strongly to the prospect of losing standing than to gaining it (Pettit, Yong and Spataro 2010, unchecked until located), and a loss of status hurts performance more when it comes from a high position than from a lower one (Marr and Thau 2014). Deference that fades with what she brings is a thinner cushion than a settled place, so this card should be the weaker of the two. Boundary, resting on common sense with adjacent support from the status-loss work, none of it replicated: where a slip would show up the very skill the others look to her for, high standing raises the cost of the slip and the card does not fire.

### E:GrpProsp->Reciprocity: Good season, open hands (Discretionary, Egalitarian)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the social psychologist's critique of VX (5 Nov 2026)

- Explanation: Whether the venture's prospects open hands around me depends on where the trouble is seen to come from. Circumstance one (the default), the trouble is inside the group or has no named source. HIGH: with the venture well within our reach I am buoyed, I help whoever asks without counting and expect the same around me, so my reading that help here comes back rises. LOW: with the venture looking beyond us, I expect everyone to hunch over their own part and help asked for to be slow to come, so my reading that help here comes back falls a little. Circumstance two, the trouble comes from outside (a rival group, lost funding, a forced change of ground), it threatens the group as a whole, and a fight still looks worth making. LOW here means the venture is threatened and not yet hopeless: I expect the others to pool what they have and help to flow to whoever needs it, so my reading that help here comes back rises. HIGH: the threat looks easily met and I expect giving to slip back to its ordinary level, so my reading eases back by no more than the threat raised it. Good mood raises helping (Carlson, Charlin and Miller 1988). Mood spreads through a group by contagion, and in one lab study groups exposed to pleasant contagion showed more cooperation and less conflict than those exposed to unpleasant contagion (Barsade 2002). That Team Confidence carries such a mood is common sense, untested by these studies. The mood evidence backs the circumstance-one increment only: a bad mood does not reliably lower helping and sometimes raises it (Carlson and Miller 1987, to be checked). The circumstance-one decrement, each member guarding her own part when the venture looks beyond the group, rests on common sense: people who doubt the shared outcome turn to securing their own. Keep it weaker than the increment, since a bad mood does not reliably lower helping. Exposure to war violence raises local cooperation, and may strengthen in-group norms in particular (Bauer et al. 2016, review, checked). In the laboratory, competition between groups raised contributions within them and sharpened anger at free riders and guilt at free riding (Puurtinen and Mappes 2009), which grounds circumstance two in rivalry between groups and not only in war; carrying either finding to an everyday outside threat such as lost funding is the model's step, so play it modest. Circumstance two needs the threat to fall on the group as a whole: where members feel singled out one by one, threat can lower cooperation within the group (Weisel and Zultan, unverified, single study; likely their 2016 European Economic Review paper on the perceived target of threat, year to be checked), so run circumstance one. Where the venture looks hopeless, run circumstance one. The step from the group's mood to her felt expectation of return rests on common sense too. Matches the rally case in E:GrpProsp->Belonging. Modest; forms a loop with E:Reciprocity->GrpProsp.

### E:Belonging->GrpProsp: Backs to each other (Reinforce, Egalitarian)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the organisational psychologist's critique (5 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When the others draw me in as one of their own, I see us talk the work through, pass on what we know and pull the same way, and the venture looks within the reach of a side like ours: my confidence that the group, as it stands, can pull it off rises.
- Decrement: When I am left at the edge, I see the work run in knots that do not talk it through or pass things on, and what I could bring left out of it, and the venture looks beyond the group as it stands: my confidence that the group can pull it off falls.
- Explanation: Cohesion is related to group performance, and all three of its components count, most for behaviour and efficiency and where work flows intensively between members (Beal et al. 2003). Nearer the target, members' felt cohesion, task cohesion above all, goes with their collective efficacy in sports teams (Paskevich et al. 1999; Kozub and McDonnell 2000; both unchecked here), though these studies are mostly cross-sectional. Where she is shut out by a group that plainly works well together, the decrement is weak or nil. ### H:GrpProsp->OwnProsp: Rising tide (Reinforce, Hierarchical)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the organisational psychologist's critique (5 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When I feel those at the top can steer us through what we have taken on, I see what I came for riding on a venture in safe hands: a well-led side that wins has places, credit and shares to hand down, and my sense that staying will bring me what I came for rises.
- Decrement: When I feel those at the top cannot steer us through what we have taken on, I see what I came for going down with a venture in the wrong hands: a badly led side has little to hand down and no name to lend me, and my sense that staying will bring me what I came for falls.
- Explanation: Deck H; the source is Team Confidence's hierarchical face. The mechanism is kept: where members' outcomes ride on the group's, the venture's prospects are part of each member's own. How fairly the top will share out what the venture yields is Parity's or Reciprocity's ground, not this card's; the card reads only how much a well- or badly-led venture will have to hand out. Where she is at the top herself, her confidence in her own steering feeds her forecast through the venture; what her rung itself brings her is H:Status->OwnProsp. A run of results can inflate or sink the face beyond the leaders' real grip, a step resting on common sense; the romance of leadership is not relied on for it, since replications failed or were mixed (Hammond et al. 2021; Schyns et al. 2025). The brake is Team Confidence evidence on the venture itself.

### H:Engagement->GrpProsp: Following a lead I believe in (Reinforce, Hierarchical)

- Increment: When I am putting much of myself into this group, I follow those at the top willingly, and what they steer us to reads as a sign of what they can do: my confidence that they can bring the venture off rises.
- Decrement: When I am pulling out of this group, I follow half-heartedly, and each misstep from the top reads as the limit of what they can do: my confidence that they can bring the venture off falls.

- Increment: When I am fond of them, I leave work in their hands and turn my back on it as I would with a friend, sure it will be carried through: my confidence that they will do their part rises.
- Decrement: When their company is a duty, I keep the work in my own hands and check what I hand over: my confidence that they will do their part falls.
- Explanation: Chimpanzees chose to trust friends far more often than non-friends in a trust game, yet friends were no more likely to return food once trusted (Engelmann and Herrmann 2016, a single small study of 15 animals; no replication found). So fondness raises her readiness to rely on them, and the card carries that felt confidence, not their reliability. The decrement rests on common sense: with little fondness she holds back that readiness. What she later sees carried through, and the sampling gain from entrusting more (Denrell 2005 is cited by Model B, not checked here), belong to the scene. Boundary: friends are trusted more without proving more trustworthy, so the card never stands in for their actual reliability; confidence built on affect predicts outcomes less well than confidence built on evidence, and its measures overlap with expectations of reliability (cited by the checker, not checked here). The card bites only on parts whose outcome she has not yet seen; a seen record outweighs it (Dependability definition), and clear evidence of parts dropped sets the increment nil. Keep the card modest. Takes over the chimpanzee evidence from E:Belonging->Dependability; where both fire, play that card small. The interaction card I:Warmth->Dependability carries the same link once the scene stages the others' side; this card carries only her own readiness to rely on them, so where the scene stages that card, play this one small. It forms a Reinforce loop with E:Dependability->Warmth; the brake is parts plainly dropped, scored by play. Evidence: provisional.

### E:Warmth->Reciprocity: Friends don't keep count (Reinforce, Egalitarian)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the comparative psychologist's second-round review (5 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When I am fond of them, I give without marking it, and a return that came late, in another kind or from another member would still count with me as help come back: my sense that help here comes back to me rises.
- Decrement: When I care little for them, I mark every favour I give, and each one not yet returned stands out as help that has not come back: my sense that help here comes back to me falls.
- Explanation: Primates reciprocate over long time frames, and the proposed mechanism is emotional bookkeeping: accumulated feeling towards partners, not a tally of who gave what (Schino and Aureli 2009). Reciprocity in food sharing may run through preferring friends (Jaeggi and Gurven 2013). People in communal relationships keep less track of inputs than people in exchange relationships (Clark 1984; Clark, Mills and Corcoran 1989; neither checked here), track the other's needs more (Clark, Mills and Powell 1986, not checked here), and like a partner less when a benefit is promptly repaid (Clark and Mills 1979). These studies concern particular partners; carrying the loose bookkeeping to the members at large, so that a return from another member counts, is the model's own step and untested. Warmth therefore slows the fall in her reading when returns lag, and lets a return from anyone count. Boundaries: the loose accounting is shown with close partners and is weaker with members she barely knows, so keep the card moderate in a large group or one new to her, and let a Close tie in the scene carry the partner-level effect. Humans reciprocate more in kind than other primates do (Jaeggi and Gurven 2013, not checked here), so a return in another kind may count for less. The card moves her reading, not the return itself; help that plainly fails to come back still lowers Reciprocity through scenes. It forms a loop with E:Reciprocity->Warmth; keep both moderate. Distinct from E:Belonging->Reciprocity (their acceptance of her) and I:Warmth->Reciprocity (friends actually giving back).

- Increment: When I feel safe to show myself, I tell them how things are with me, the good news as well as the knocks, and in the telling I find myself drawn to the ones I have told: my fondness for them rises.
- Decrement: When I brace and mask myself, I keep my news and my troubles to myself and sit through their talk at arm's length, and the distance I keep settles into a coolness towards them: my fondness for them falls a little.
- Explanation: People tell others about most of their emotional episodes, and sharing builds bonds between teller and listener (Rimé 2009, review); the card takes from this that the telling itself draws the teller closer. Warmth reads the members as a whole, so scale the rise to how much of the group she has told; telling one friend moves it little. Keep the decrement smaller: masking keeps her from growing closer more than it pushes her away. The rise comes over the minutes of the telling. Forms a loop with E:Warmth->Safety.

### E:Prestige->Warmth: Passed over for her (Reinforce, Egalitarian)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the affective scientist's second-round review (5 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When the others turn to me whenever my kind of skill counts, no one is preferred over me in it, and I warm to those who look to me: my fondness for them rises a little.
- Decrement: When they turn past me to another member for my kind of skill, I feel the sting; where she plainly earned it, my fondness barely moves, and where she seems not to have earned it the sting turns sour and I resent her and those who turn to her: my fondness for them falls.
- Explanation: Envy comes in two forms with different experiences and aims: benign envy moves the envier to rise, malicious envy to pull the other down (van de Ven, Zeelenberg and Pieters 2009, three countries); the two are separate dispositions, with hope for success feeding the benign form and fear of failure the malicious (Lange and Crusius 2015). Whether the other's advantage seems deserved is the appraisal that separates them (van de Ven, Zeelenberg and Pieters 2012). The two-type split is itself contested (Cohen-Charash and Larson 2017, not checked here), so lean on the deservingness appraisal more than on the labels. Size the decrement by that appraisal. Where the rival plainly does it better, the envy is benign: I watch how the rival does it and want to match her, and my fondness falls only a little or not at all. Where the rival seems not to have earned it (show, or luck the others mistake for skill), the envy is malicious and the fall is larger, aimed at the rival and those who turn to her. Where the others turn to the rival as a favourite, tilting chances or a hearing towards their own, that is Parity's ground and cools fondness through E:Parity->Warmth; play this card small there. The increment is small: being looked to for her own kind of skill leaves the focal member with no one preferred over her in it and warms her a little to those who look to her, which rests on common sense. A close other's success threatens most when it lies in one's own domain (Tesser 1988), so scale the card to how near the rival is to the focal member and how much the skill is the focal member's own. Warmth is fondness for the members at large, so scale the fall to how much of the group the rival and those who turn to her make up. In a small band where a would-be star is levelled by ribbing, the unearned case also feeds the talk carried by X:Esteem->Repute when the pack is on; this card moves only the focal member's fondness. The card's era rests on prestige-biased copying, deference to those whose skill is worth learning from (Henrich and Gil-White 2001, not checked here); capuchin inequity aversion is contested and does not carry it. Envy is felt within seconds of being passed over; its cooling settles over the gathering.

### E:GrpProsp->Warmth: Going well, glad of them (Reinforce, Egalitarian)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the affective scientist's second-round review (5 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When I feel surer we can pull the venture off, the work runs light, I laugh more easily with them and am glad to be in their company: my fondness for them rises a little.
- Decrement: When the venture starts to look beyond us, the gatherings turn tense and flat and their company weighs on me: my fondness for them falls a little.
- Explanation: Members of successful groups tend to like one another more than members of unsuccessful ones (Lott and Lott 1965, a review of group cohesiveness as interpersonal attraction), so a lift in the venture's prospects warms her to the members and a sag cools her. That evidence concerns outcomes received; the step from her felt confidence in the venture is by analogy. Affect tracks the rate of progress towards a goal against what was expected (Carver and Scheier 1990, searched). In one lab study, groups exposed to pleasant mood contagion showed more cooperation and less conflict than those exposed to unpleasant contagion (Barsade 2002), a supporting route through shared mood. Performance and cohesion are linked both ways; Mullen and Copper (1994, cited by Model B, not checked here) find commitment to the task the main part, while Beal et al. (2003, not checked here) find interpersonal attraction related to performance too, so the share carried by liking is not settled. Ratings of cohesion after performance feedback may be partly halo. Keep the card small. Boundary: the same review finds that shared failure can also raise liking, especially where it is imposed arbitrarily from outside (Lott and Lott 1965); where the venture sags under an outside blow they all bear, set the decrement nil, and fondness may hold or rise in play. Warmth here reads the members as a whole. Fire it on a change in Team Confidence, not on a steady level: a venture long known to be in reach lifts no one. Distinct from E:Team Confidence->Belonging, which carries the others' ease with her.

### X:NormExp->OutResp: Pulled up, back in line (Reinforce, X)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the behavioural economist's second-round review (6 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When letting a part drop jars me as plainly wrong, whoever does it, and it would show in me, by a word, a mutter or a cold shoulder, I know a part let drop here will not slip by unnoticed, by me at least, and I leave work in their hands with less checking: my confidence that they will do their part rises a little.
- Decrement: When letting a part drop leaves me unmoved and I would let it pass, I know nothing I do holds anyone to their part, so a part left with them rests more on their goodwill and I start keeping an eye on it: my confidence that they will do their part falls a little.
- Explanation: The chance to sanction raises and holds contributions (Fehr and Gächter 2000), disapproval that costs the target nothing works too, though less than fines (Masclet et al. 2003), and across 187 effect sizes punishment raised cooperation by about d = 0.7, more in groups that stay together (Balliet, Mulder and Van Lange 2011). Dependability may rest on knowing that a part let drop gets pulled up by the group or its rules. The card carries her own side of that: where a breach jars her, she counts on a dropped part not slipping by; where it leaves her unmoved, her reliance rests more on the others' goodwill. The evidence above makes that expectation a fair one; the step from her own bristle to her confidence in others rests on common sense. A high reading does not by itself mean she will speak up: whether the bristle comes out depends on her power and what she stands to lose (Molho et al. 2020), so scale the increment to whether she could voice or show it; a low member who would bristle and keep quiet gains little. Whether the others then carry their parts belongs to the scene (rulebook 8.5) and moves Dependability through its own plays, so keep both lines small. Where the scene stages the others backing or ignoring her objection, play I:Rule-Keeping->Dependability instead. Where a part has just been dropped in the scene, the dropped part lowers Dependability through its own play; this card only softens that fall for a member whose bristle would show, and never turns a dodge into a net rise. Boundaries. Sanctions do more in high-trust settings (Balliet and Van Lange 2013, not checked here): scale the increment to how far she already trusts the members. Lab punishment effects may not carry over to small egalitarian field groups (Guala 2012, not checked here), which keeps the card small. Where antisocial punishment or counter-punishment is common, pulling up draws retaliation and less cooperation (Herrmann, Thöni and Gächter 2008; Nikiforakis 2008), and she knows her bristle promises little: set the increment small. Where she cannot see who dropped what, her bristle may land on the wrong member and cooperation can fall (Grechenig, Nicklisch and Thöni 2010): set both lines small. Punishment pays on long horizons and can cost on short ones (Gächter, Renner and Sefton 2008, searched): larger in a standing group, small for a one-off task. A standing sanction can lower trust that others act from their own will, and reliance then drops further once the check is gone (Mulder, van Dijk, De Cremer and Wilke 2006); this crowding-out is contested, with a 2023 preregistered replication reported to find no effect (cited by the checker, not checked here), so where she reads the others as carrying parts only because they are watched, set the increment small, and treat the boundary as provisional. Where the others think the breach harmless (H:Rule-Keeping->Belonging, circumstance two), set both small. A lone rebuke she expects no one to take up bites less, so scale the increment to whether she expects others to join. Distinct from E:Parity->Dependability: compliance under one coercer's eye is not this card. With X:Dependability->Rule-Keeping it forms a Reinforce loop: parts carried keep a dodge conspicuous and her bristle keen, and a keen bristle keeps her counting on parts being carried. The loop runs down as readily as up, so keep both small and watch for runs from play to play. Deck X: Rule-Keeping is hierarchical, Dependability egalitarian.

### X:Levelling->OutResp: Rules we had a say in (Reinforce, X)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the behavioural economist's second-round review (6 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When those above hear us out before setting how the work is shared, hold everyone at our rung to the same rules and keep to them themselves, I expect the others to take the rules as their own and carry their parts without being chased: my confidence that they will do their part rises.
- Decrement: When the rules for the work are set over our heads and bent for favourites, I expect the others to do the least the rules demand and let parts slide once no one is looking: my confidence that they will do their part falls.
- Explanation: A rule to promote cooperation raised cooperation more when subjects voted for it than when it was imposed, with selection controlled (Dal Bó, Foster and Putterman 2010); Ugandan farmers cooperated more under an elected monitor than a randomly chosen one (Baldassarri and Grossman 2011). Leaders who contribute first shape followers' early beliefs (Gächter and Renner 2018), which bears directly on the clause that those above keep to the rules themselves. Ostrom's principles that members help make the rules and that monitors answer to them are well supported in 91 field studies (Cox, Arnold and Villamayor-Tomás 2010). Apart from the leader clause, the step from the others' behaviour in those findings to her own confidence in them is inferred, resting on what she expects of them and on common sense; keep it moderate. Boundaries: a say in the rules helps only where members adopt them and are bound by them, and choosing institutions is not reliably better than having them imposed (Dannenberg and Gallier 2020, not checked here). The favouritism clause has only small, unreplicated lab support, so play the decrement's bent-rules case small. Reads Parity's hierarchical face; E:Levelling->OutResp reads coercion among equals, so play only the card for the face in play. Deck X: Parity's H face to an egalitarian Pillar.

### E:Warmth->Belonging: Fond, so wanted (Reinforce, Egalitarian)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the sociologist's second-round review (9 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When I am fond of them, I take it they are fond of me too, and a nod, a seat kept or a glance my way reads as warmth returned: my sense that they want me among them rises.
- Decrement: When their company is a duty to me, I take it the coolness runs both ways, and the same nod reads as politeness and a gap beside me as meant: my sense that they want me among them falls a little.
- Explanation: Assumed reciprocity of liking: a perceiver who likes a target tends to think the target likes her back, and this is among the strongest effects in interpersonal perception; with actual reciprocity of liking it accounts for most of how accurately people know who likes them (Kenny and DePaulo 1993). Her fondness therefore colours how she reads the small, ambiguous signs of acceptance. The card is her projection only; whether they in fact draw her in is the interaction card I:Warmth->Belonging, so on one cue play one. It acts on ambiguous signs; plain welcome or plain exclusion is read as it is. People underestimate how much they are liked early in acquaintance (the liking gap: Boothby et al. 2018), so keep the increment modest for a newcomer. Keep the decrement smaller than the increment: under the E face a reserved member stays one of them. With E:Belonging->Warmth it forms a tight Reinforce loop; the brake is plain exclusion read as it is. Where E:Engagement->Belonging also colours the same cue, play one. Reads generalised assumed reciprocity (Kenny and DePaulo 1993): fondness for the members as a whole colours her sense of being wanted by them as a whole; fondness for one partner belongs to interaction cards. Evidence: provisional; the step from assumed reciprocity to her felt acceptance by the group rests on common sense.

### H:Levelling->NormExp: Rules worth keeping (Reinforce, Hierarchical)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the sociologist's second-round review (9 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When those above hear me out, hold everyone at my rung to the same rules and keep to them themselves, the settled ways read as ours and rightly kept, and a member who breaks them in a way the group would condemn jars me as plainly wrong: my indignation at breaches rises.
- Decrement: When calls are made over my head and the rules bend for favourites, the settled ways read as theirs, kept for their own benefit, and a member who slips the group's conventions stirs little in me: my indignation at breaches falls.
- Explanation: Fair procedure from authorities raises the legitimacy members grant them, a path that holds in longitudinal data, and legitimacy raises compliance (Walters and Bolger 2019, meta-analysis); in the group engagement model fair procedure feeds members' identity and through it their rule following (Tyler and Blader 2003). That evidence concerns her own endorsement and compliance. Adjacent evidence links legitimacy to acting against others' breaches: people who judge the police legitimate are readier to report crimes and help bring offenders in (Sunshine and Tyler 2003; Tyler and Fagan 2008; neither checked here). That evidence is behaviour, not felt indignation, so the step to her indignation at another member's breach rests on common sense: one guards ways one holds rightful and shrugs at breaches of ways kept for others' benefit. No direct test found, a gap. Boundaries: where the breach is a refusal of the very rule that serves the favourites, she may side with the breacher (normative conflict, as on H:Engagement->NormExp), so the card does not fire there. The decrement acts on the group's conventions only: a breach that wrongs a member, taking from her, hurting her or leaving her to carry the breacher's share, still jars her however the ways are kept from above, and the card does not soften it. The decrement concerns breaches by members at large; a favourite let off the rules is the tilt itself, read on Parity, and the card does not lower her bristle at it. Fair procedure also raises identification, which reaches Rule-Keeping through H:Engagement->NormExp; where the Engagement update already carries the same treatment, play that card small. Rule-Keeping here is her indignation at others' breaches, not her own conformity. Modest.

### E:OutResp->Reciprocity: Till we both have ours (Reinforce, Egalitarian)

Status: provisional. Origin: Proposed by the developmental psychologist's second-round review (9 Nov 2026)

- Increment: When the others carry through what they take on and stay with a shared task until it is done, I expect them to stay with me until my share of it is in hand, and to come when I ask a hand with the next one: my sense that help here comes back to me rises.
- Decrement: When the others drop what they take on and drift off once their own part is paid, I expect them to be gone when I need a hand to finish mine, and an ask of mine to go unanswered: my sense that help here comes back to me falls.
- Explanation: By three, children who have made a joint commitment protest when a partner breaks off and acknowledge their own leaving (Grafenhain, Behne, Carpenter and Tomasello 2009), and they keep working at a joint task after their own reward is in hand until the partner has theirs, less so when the task was not joint (Hamann, Warneken and Tomasello 2012). Where E:Engagement->Reciprocity circumstance one fires on the same scene, play this card small. Forms a Reinforce loop with E:Reciprocity->OutResp; the brake is a need plainly met or unmet, scored by play.
